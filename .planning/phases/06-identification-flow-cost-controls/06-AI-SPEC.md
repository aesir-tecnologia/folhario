## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Extraction (structured top-3 species classification from 1..N user-uploaded plant photos, model-agnostic, two REST API providers behind a locked adapter)

**Description:**

A vision-based plant species identifier for Brazilian beginners who just bought their first plant and have no idea what it is. The user uploads 1..N photos via a mobile-first PWA; the backend dispatches them through a locked adapter to either Plant.id (Kindwise, primary, domain-tuned vision classifier) or an OpenAI-compatible vision LLM (fallback) and returns a ranked top-3 of `{ scientificName, commonName (pt-BR or null), confidence }`. "Good" means: (a) the correct species — at the level a Brazilian beginner can act on — appears in the top-3 for everyday Brazilian houseplants and common natives; (b) confidence is honestly calibrated, not marketing-inflated, and the UI's confidence ladder reflects real uncertainty (PRD §17 anti-fake-precision); (c) the pt-BR `commonName` is either a name a Brazilian would actually use ("comigo-ninguém-pode", "espada-de-São-Jorge", "costela-de-Adão") or `null` — never a fabricated translation; (d) the user understands they are sending a photo to a third-party AI provider abroad (LGPD Art. 33), and that disclosure happens before the first call, not after.

**Critical Failure Modes:**

<!-- The 3-5 behaviors that absolutely cannot go wrong in this system -->

1. **Toxic look-alike misidentified as benign at high confidence.** A Dieffenbachia ("comigo-ninguém-pode") returned confidently as a non-toxic aroid (e.g., a Philodendron variety treated as harmless), or vice-versa, leading a household with children or pets to under-protect a plant whose calcium-oxalate raphides routinely send Brazilian under-5s to poison control. The wrong species selection cascades into Phase 7's care guide, which never surfaces the toxicity warning that should have been there. Brazilian poison-control data shows ~70% of Dieffenbachia incidents involve children under 5; this is not a hypothetical edge case.
2. **Fabricated pt-BR common name.** The OpenAI-compat fallback invents a Portuguese-sounding common name that does not exist in any Brazilian botanical registry (SiBBr / Reflora / Flora e Funga do Brasil) when the species genuinely has no Brazilian vernacular. Beginners trust the name, search for it, find nothing, and lose trust in the app. Plant.id with `?language=pt` returns `commonName: null` safely; the fabrication risk is concentrated on the LLM provider and is mitigated only by the system-prompt clause "do NOT invent one" — defense in depth requires eval coverage, not just a prompt instruction.
3. **Over-confident wrong answer in the high-confidence band (≥0.70).** A wrong species shown above the "Alta confiança" threshold of the UI confidence ladder violates the PRD §17 anti-fake-precision contract directly. The confidence ladder is the user's only honesty signal; if the band is unreliable it is worse than no band, because beginners over-trust the green-state result and skip the "Não é nenhuma destas?" manual-correction affordance.
4. **Native-vs-ornamental confusion that cascades to wrong care guidance.** A Brazilian native (e.g., Anthurium / Heliconia / a Bromeliad genus, Pau-d'Água as Dracaena fragrans) is identified as a foreign cultivar with the same silhouette but different humidity / light / substrate needs — or vice-versa. The user lands in their catalog with a Plant→Species link that drives Phase 7's care guide and Phase 8's reminders toward the wrong watering cadence; plant death within 30 days, churn, blame on the app.
5. **Non-plant input returned as a confident plant.** A photo of furniture, a printed plant pattern on fabric, or a plastic decorative plant is identified as Sansevieria/Dracaena trifasciata or any other species at high confidence. There is no "this isn't a plant" output in the contract — the model is forced to pick a species — so the only defenses are confidence calibration and `min_confidence=0.30` filtering. Eval must include negative-class examples to verify the system collapses to zero-results rather than confidently classifying a chair as a Costela-de-Adão.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Consumer plant care / horticulture / mobile gardening apps — Brazilian market. Comparable products: PictureThis (Glority), PlantNet (CIRAD-led, citizen-science-backed), Garden Answers, Seek by iNaturalist. Closest functional analogue is PictureThis (closed-model, beginner-targeted, monetised); closest accuracy-honesty analogue is PlantNet (academic, transparent about per-organ confidence and "no result" states). Folhário targets a niche neither serves well — pt-BR-first, Brazilian-flora-aware, LGPD-compliant.

**User Population:** Brazilian beginners with their first plant. Mobile-first (PWA on Android-majority hardware). pt-BR monolingual or pt-BR-dominant. Low botanical literacy — most cannot tell a Monstera from a Rhaphidophora, do not know the difference between "espécie" and "cultivar", and have never heard the words "calcium oxalate" or "raphide". Low tolerance for jargon (Latin scientific names land cold; pt-BR common names are the trust currency). Low trust in AI generally and high churn risk on first failure: if the first identification fails or feels dishonest, they do not return. Household context routinely includes children under 5 and pets — making toxicity correctness a household-safety concern, not just a horticultural one.

**Stakes Level:** **Medium** — wrong identification cascades to wrong care guidance (Phase 7) and wrong watering cadence (Phase 8), with plant death within ~30 days the realistic downside. **Critical for the toxic-look-alike subset** — a confident misidentification of a Dieffenbachia as a non-toxic aroid (or the reverse) in a household with toddlers or cats has real medical consequences. Folhário is not a regulated medical or veterinary product, but the toxicity edge case is a household-safety case the eval rubric must cover explicitly.

**Output Consequence:** A selected top-3 result becomes a `Plant` row in the user's catalog linked to a `Species`, which gates (a) Phase 7's pt-BR care guide rendering, (b) Phase 8's watering reminder cadence, and (c) any future toxicity / pet-safety surfacing. A wrong species at this gate poisons every downstream artifact silently — there is no manual reconciliation surface beyond the "Não é nenhuma destas?" link at selection time and the re-associate-with-catalog action in history. The user's mental model is "the app told me what plant I have" — they will not second-guess the species name when the reminder fires three days later.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Scientific name correctness (genus + species)**
- Good (expert accepts): Genus and species are both correct against the Flora e Funga do Brasil / Reflora canonical list (or GBIF backbone for non-native ornamentals). Modern accepted name preferred — e.g., `Dracaena trifasciata` (current, post-APG IV 2016) over the legacy `Sansevieria trifasciata`, though either is acceptable as long as the species concept is right.
- Bad (expert flags): Wrong genus (Monstera ↔ Philodendron ↔ Rhaphidophora confusion); right genus but wrong species (e.g., `Monstera deliciosa` vs. `Monstera adansonii` — different leaf, different growth habit, different care); fabricated/non-existent binomial.
- Stakes: High (cascades to care guide).
- Source: Hart et al. 2023 (PLOS One/Wiley) — peer-reviewed evaluation of plant ID apps reports ~70% species-level accuracy at high classification scores for PlantNet; family/genus accuracy >85%. The genus-level threshold is the realistic floor; sub-genus discrimination is where commercial apps regularly fail and where Brazilian beginners cannot self-correct.

**Dimension: pt-BR common name authenticity**
- Good (expert accepts): The `commonName` is one a Brazilian would actually use — present in SiBBr / Flora e Funga do Brasil vernacular indices, or attested in Brazilian horticultural trade. Examples: "comigo-ninguém-pode" (Dieffenbachia seguine), "espada-de-São-Jorge" (Dracaena trifasciata, regardless of taxonomy update — Brazilians keep the name), "costela-de-Adão" (Monstera deliciosa), "pau-d'água" (Dracaena fragrans). When no Brazilian common name exists, `null` is the correct answer.
- Bad (expert flags): Portuguese-from-Portugal name where a distinct pt-BR name exists; English name lightly transliterated ("planta-cobra" calqued from "snake plant" when "espada-de-São-Jorge" is the universal pt-BR name); a fabricated pt-BR name that exists in no Brazilian registry; or a non-null name that exists but refers to a different species in Brazilian usage.
- Stakes: High (this is the user's primary trust signal — Latin names land cold).
- Source: SiBBr / Reflora / Lista de Espécies da Flora e Funga do Brasil (Jardim Botânico do Rio de Janeiro). Practitioner standard: a name is "real" if a Brazilian nursery or paisagista would write it on a plant tag.

**Dimension: Honest uncertainty (calibration)**
- Good (expert accepts): When the photo is ambiguous (single leaf, blurry, partial, similar genus with no distinguishing feature visible), confidence is below the "high" threshold (<0.70) or the system returns zero results above `min_confidence=0.30`. When the photo is clear and the species is common, confidence is high. The confidence number does not float in the 0.40–0.69 "medium" band as a default hedge.
- Bad (expert flags): A Rhaphidophora tetrasperma returned at 0.91 confidence as Monstera deliciosa from a single juvenile-leaf photo (a known beginner trap that even commercial apps fall into). Confidence inflated to push the user into a selection. All results clustered at 0.85–0.95 regardless of input quality (uncalibrated softmax).
- Stakes: Critical (PRD §17 anti-fake-precision is a launch blocker).
- Source: PRD §17 + Hart et al. 2023 — confidence calibration is the dimension where commercial apps most consistently fail; the confidence ladder is meaningless if its bands are noise.

**Dimension: Top-3 ranking quality (recall over precision)**
- Good (expert accepts): The correct species is in the top-3 even if not at #1, for common Brazilian houseplants and common natives. Beginners pick visually from the cards; #2 and #3 are real options, not throwaway distractors. Equal-prominence layout (D-24) means the user is not nudged toward #1 by visual hierarchy.
- Bad (expert flags): Correct species absent from top-3 when present in the photo; #2 and #3 are clearly off-genus filler that no expert would consider; ranking inversion where confidence numbers do not match relative likelihood.
- Stakes: High (the manual-correction path is friction; top-3 recall is the primary success metric).
- Source: Hart et al. 2023 reports top-1 vs. top-3 accuracy gaps of 10–15 percentage points across major apps — top-3 is the honest evaluation grain for vision classifiers, not top-1.

**Dimension: Toxicity-relevant species discrimination**
- Good (expert accepts): Within the aroid family (Araceae — the genus cluster Brazilians most commonly own and most commonly mis-handle), Dieffenbachia spp. are not confused with non-toxic look-alikes, and vice-versa. The species selection lands somewhere whose toxicity profile matches reality at the genus level at minimum.
- Bad (expert flags): Dieffenbachia seguine returned as a Philodendron variety the user assumes is harmless (or vice-versa); any toxic species mis-genus'd into a non-toxic one with high confidence; missing the family entirely on a textbook Dieffenbachia photo.
- Stakes: Critical (household safety — Brazilian poison-control data: ~70% of Dieffenbachia incidents are children <5).
- Source: Brazilian veterinary toxicology literature (Pubvet / OBSERVATÓRIO DE LA ECONOMÍA LATINOAMERICANA case reports); Poison Control (US National Capital) on Dieffenbachia/Philodendron raphide mechanism; CONSULT veterinary toxicologist for the canonical Brazilian "must not be confused" pair list.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Aroid-family confusion (the beginner trap).** Monstera deliciosa, Monstera adansonii, Philodendron bipinnatifidum, Rhaphidophora tetrasperma, and various Epipremnum / Scindapsus all have overlapping silhouettes and shared informal common names ("Swiss Cheese Plant" applies to at least three genera; "Mini Monstera" is a marketing name for a non-Monstera). Commercial apps and beginners both fail here. Care requirements diverge enough (support, watering, pruning) that a Rhaphidophora treated as a Monstera will under-grow or die back. Source: <https://jordansjungle.net/blogs/news/how-are-they-different-philodendron-monstera-epipremnum-scindapsus-rhaphidophora>; <https://lifetips.alibaba.com/plant-care/monstera-look-alike>.

2. **Sansevieria → Dracaena legacy taxonomy confusion.** APG IV (2016) reclassified Sansevieria into Dracaena; `Sansevieria trifasciata` is now `Dracaena trifasciata`. Plant.id and the OpenAI-compat provider may return either name; nurseries in Brazil are mid-transition; users universally call it "espada-de-São-Jorge" regardless. The eval should accept either binomial as correct as long as the species concept matches; the pt-BR common name should remain "espada-de-São-Jorge". Source: APG IV / Wikipedia Sansevieria; GBIF Sansevieria trifasciata Prain; <https://homeplantbot.com/snake-plant-sansevieria-vs-dracaena-the-reclassification-explained/>.

3. **Succulent look-alikes from rosette-only photos.** Echeveria, Sempervivum, Graptopetalum, Pachyphytum, and × Graptoveria hybrids overlap heavily from a top-down rosette shot. Beginners cannot distinguish; vision models often cannot from a single angle. Confidence in the medium band is the honest answer; high confidence on a rosette-only shot is a calibration failure.

4. **Brazilian-native genus confusion.** Anthurium spp., Heliconia spp., and the Bromeliad genera (Aechmea, Neoregelia, Vriesea, Guzmania, Tillandsia) all sit in territory where vision models trained predominantly on European/North American houseplant data can underperform. The "right answer in top-3" bar matters more here than top-1.

5. **Cultivar / variegation invisibility.** Monstera "Thai Constellation" vs. plain `Monstera deliciosa`, variegated "Albo" Monsteras, golden Pothos vs. marble queen — care is similar within a species but pricing, expectations, and propagation behaviour are dramatically different. Returning the species without the cultivar is acceptable for MVP; returning a wrong cultivar at high confidence is worse than no cultivar.

6. **Toxic vs. non-toxic look-alike at the family level.** Dieffenbachia seguine (toxic, common Brazilian houseplant, commonly with children/pets in proximity) vs. similar-looking non-toxic aroids. Symptoms in pets: hypersalivation, oral swelling, dysphagia. ~70% of human Dieffenbachia poisonings are children under 5. This is the domain failure mode with the highest non-horticultural stakes. Source: <https://www.poison.org/articles/dieffenbachia-and-philodendron-202>; <https://ojs.observatoriolatinoamericano.com/ojs/index.php/olel/article/view/5559> (Brazilian veterinary case report); <https://www.correiobraziliense.com.br/cbradar/a-planta-toxica-que-muitos-tem-na-sala-sem-saber/>.

7. **Diseased / damaged plant throws species ID off entirely.** Yellowed leaves, fungal spots, sun-scorched edges, mealybug damage — all common reasons a beginner reaches for the app — visually distort the features the model relies on. The honest output is lower confidence or a no-result; a confident wrong species is worse than admitting uncertainty.

8. **Non-plant inputs.** Photos of furniture with a plant-print pattern, plastic plants, a plant on a magazine cover, or a leaf-shaped object — there is no "not a plant" output in the contract, and the `min_confidence=0.30` filter is the only safety net. Eval must include negative-class examples.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

- **LGPD Art. 33 — international transfer disclosure (binding, in scope this phase).** Plant.id is operated by Kindwise (Czech Republic, EU/EEA — relevant adequacy posture differs from the US transfer); the OpenAI-compat fallback is US-hosted. Both transfers require explicit, informed, granular consent BEFORE the first call. The consent modal (D-16/D-17) names both providers explicitly per LGPD transparency principle; a vendor swap requires `policy_version` bump + re-consent.
- **LGPD treatment of user-uploaded photos as personal data.** Plant photos are personal data under LGPD when associated with an identified user (via JWT subject); the EXIF/GPS strip (client-side per CLAUDE.md image pipeline + server defense-in-depth per D-28 / IDENT-16) is the necessity/minimisation control. Photos are NOT biometric data in the LGPD sense (no facial biometric template extracted) — the heightened biometric regime in the ANPD's 2026-2027 priority map does not apply.
- **ANPD 2026-2027 regulatory hot zone — AI transparency.** ANPD's published 2026-2027 inspection priorities include AI systems' transparency, bias mitigation, and rights of data subjects. Folhário's identification flow IS an AI system processing personal data (the photo); the consent modal language and the public LGPD policy must clearly disclose the AI nature, the providers, and the retention posture. This is not a new obligation — it is heightened regulatory attention on existing LGPD principles. Source: <https://teletime.com.br/23/01/2026/anpd-define-75-fiscalizacoes-para-bienio-2026-2027-confira-prioridades/>; <https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/documentos-tecnicos-orientativos/radar-tecnologico-biometria-anpd-1.pdf>.
- **No specific Brazilian botanical-identification regulation.** Identifying a plant species from a user photo carries no domain-specific regulatory regime in Brazil for an MVP at this scale. // VERIFY: IBAMA / ICMBio reporting obligations for endangered-species photos (e.g., Pau-Brasil, Araucaria angustifolia) — likely N/A for an MVP that does not transmit GPS, but worth a one-line legal review before launch.
- **Toxicity disclosure NOT regulated as a medical product.** Folhário is not a medical, veterinary, or first-aid product. Surfacing toxicity information in care guides (Phase 7 territory) is a product safety / consumer expectation matter, not an ANVISA-regulated one.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** No AI framework — direct vendor SDKs under the locked `IdentificationProvider` adapter (D-01/D-02). Plant.id connector uses native `fetch` + Zod 4 validation; OpenAI-compat connector uses `openai` SDK with `baseURL` override + Zod 4 on the parsed response.

**Version:** `openai` ^5.x; `zod` ^4.3.6 (already in deps); native `fetch` (Node 22+ on Vercel runtime).

**Rationale:**

Phase 6 architecture is already locked at exactly the layer a framework would try to own:

- **D-01/D-02** lock the `IdentificationProvider` adapter interface and connector layout
- **D-05** locks atomic preflight cost reservation in a single Postgres transaction BEFORE any provider HTTP call
- **D-06** locks `AbortController` propagation through the connector to the underlying `fetch` call (50s outer / 30s per-call / ≥10s remaining-budget guard)
- **D-08–D-10** lock a DB-backed circuit breaker with specific fault-counting semantics
- **D-31** locks per-test injected mock connectors via `__setProviderForTests` test seam

Any framework that owns the call site (LangChain, LangGraph, OpenAI Agents SDK, Vercel AI SDK as a primary) would conflict with these locks and add abstraction surface CLAUDE.md and D-01 explicitly minimized. The use case is one-shot structured image classification with two REST APIs — no chain, no graph, no agent, no retrieval, no tool use. Direct SDKs are the honest answer.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
| --------- | ----------------- |
| Vercel AI SDK 5+ (`generateObject({ schema })`) | Marginal upside over `openai` SDK + Zod for vision-response parsing; cannot unify Plant.id (non-LLM REST taxonomy API), so the `IdentificationProvider` adapter remains required regardless. May be reconsidered _inside_ the OpenAI-compat connector only if Zod-based parsing proves brittle. |
| LangChain / LangGraph | Anti-pattern #1 in `ai-frameworks.md`. No chain, no graph, no agent — adds abstraction overhead with no payoff. Owns call site, conflicts with D-05/D-06 locks. |
| OpenAI Agents SDK | Designed for agentic delegation (Handoffs, Guardrails, Tracing); we need a single classification call, not an agent. Locks vendor (we need fallover to a different vendor by design — D-04). |
| LlamaIndex | RAG-first; Phase 6 has no retrieval, no documents, no vector store. Wrong tool. |
| CrewAI | Multi-agent orchestration; system is single-call extraction, not collaborative agents. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No — the entire point of D-04 (priority ordering) and the adapter pattern is to keep both providers swappable. Plant.id today, OpenAI-compat as fallback, and the door is open to swap either for a Brazil-friendly third (e.g., a domestic Pix-billed vision API) without touching use-cases.

### Entry Point Pattern

The `IdentificationProvider` interface (D-01) and both connector skeletons:

```typescript
// src/shared/adapters/identification-provider.ts (D-02)
import type { z } from 'zod';

export type IdentificationInput = {
  photos: Array<{ bytes: Buffer; mime: string }>;  // 1..N, post-validateIdentificationPhotos
  signal: AbortSignal;                             // D-06: per-call 30s child controller
  // No prompt or system override — versioned with deploy per IDENT-19
};

export type NormalizedResult = {
  results: Array<{
    scientificName: string;   // italic display per PRD §17
    commonName: string | null; // pt-BR preferred; null when provider has no pt-BR entry
    confidence: number;       // 0..1
  }>;
};

export type IdentifyOutcome = {
  canonical: NormalizedResult;
  raw: unknown;               // persisted to Identification.results jsonb per D-03
};

export interface IdentificationProvider {
  readonly providerId: string;
  readonly model: string;
  readonly costPerCallCents: number;             // for ProviderUsageCounter accounting
  estimateCost(input: IdentificationInput): number; // cents — feeds D-05 preflight reservation
  identify(input: IdentificationInput): Promise<IdentifyOutcome>;
}

// src/contexts/identification/infrastructure/providers/plant-id.ts
const PlantIdResponseSchema = z.object({
  result: z.object({
    classification: z.object({
      suggestions: z.array(
        z.object({
          name: z.string(),                       // scientific name
          probability: z.number().min(0).max(1),
          details: z
            .object({
              common_names: z.array(z.string()).nullable().optional(),
            })
            .optional(),
        }),
      ),
    }),
  }),
});

export class PlantIdProvider implements IdentificationProvider {
  readonly providerId = 'plant-id';
  readonly model = 'plant-id-v3';
  readonly costPerCallCents = 6;                  // ~€0.05 entry tier; tune from ProviderBudget seed

  constructor(private readonly apiKey: string) {}

  estimateCost(_input: IdentificationInput): number {
    return this.costPerCallCents;                 // Plant.id is flat-rate per identification credit
  }

  async identify(input: IdentificationInput): Promise<IdentifyOutcome> {
    const images = input.photos.map(
      (p) => `data:${p.mime};base64,${p.bytes.toString('base64')}`,
    );
    // VERIFY against current Plant.id v3 docs — endpoint + query params as of May 2026
    const url = new URL('https://api.plant.id/v3/identification');
    url.searchParams.set('details', 'common_names');
    url.searchParams.set('language', 'pt');       // pt-BR common-name preference (binding domain req)
    const res = await fetch(url, {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: { 'Api-Key': this.apiKey, 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
      body: JSON.stringify({
        images,
        classification_level: 'species',
        similar_images: false,
      }),
      signal: input.signal,                       // D-06: propagate AbortSignal to underlying fetch
    });
    if (!res.ok) throw new Error(`plant-id ${res.status}`);
    const json: unknown = await res.json();
    const parsed = PlantIdResponseSchema.parse(json); // throws on novel shape → invalid_response (D-15)
    return {
      canonical: {
        results: parsed.result.classification.suggestions.map((s) => ({
          scientificName: s.name,
          commonName: s.details?.common_names?.[0] ?? null,  // null when no pt-BR entry
          confidence: s.probability,
        })),
      },
      raw: json,
    };
  }

  'You are a botanical classifier. The user uploads 1..N photos of ONE plant.',
  'Return ONLY the JSON specified by the schema. Up to 3 species candidates,',
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
| ------- | ---------- | --------------- |
| `IdentificationProvider` adapter (D-01) | Single TS interface — `identify` + `estimateCost` + static metadata | Every new vendor connector implements it; router consumes it; tests inject mocks via D-31 seam |
| `NormalizedResult` (D-03) | Vendor-agnostic shape `{ results: [{ scientificName, commonName, confidence }] }` | UI filtering / sorting / threshold check (IDENT-04); `raw` audit trail persisted alongside |
| `AbortSignal` propagation (D-06) | One outer 50s controller + one 30s per-call child; both signals threaded into `fetch`/`openai.chat.completions.parse({}, { signal })` | Every provider call. Without it, network reads keep running after the wall-clock budget expires |
| `estimateCost(input)` (D-01) | Per-call cents estimate — flat for Plant.id, photo-count derived for OpenAI-compat | Feeds the atomic preflight reservation (D-05) BEFORE any HTTP call |
| Atomic preflight reservation (D-05) | One short transaction: conditional `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` only when `current + estimate <= ceiling` | Once per attempt, before connector.identify(). Zero rows updated → mark provider exhausted, try next |
| Circuit breaker state (D-08) | Module-level `Map<provider, BreakerState>` fronting `provider_breaker_states` table; same TTL as budget cache | Read before each call to skip-open providers; written on consecutive-failure threshold trip |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Forgetting to forward `AbortSignal` into the underlying `fetch`** — A 30s parent timeout cancels nothing if the connector never plumbed `signal` into the `fetch` call. The result is zombie HTTP requests that keep the Vercel function alive past the 50s budget and burn provider quota for results no one will read. Plumb `input.signal` into BOTH the native `fetch({ signal })` AND `openai.chat.completions.parse(params, { signal })` (the SDK takes signal as the SECOND argument; passing it inside the params object is silently ignored).
2. **Confusing the OpenAI SDK's `timeout` option with `AbortSignal`** — The SDK's per-request `timeout` is a client-side fetch timer. It does not satisfy D-06 (which requires the outer 50s wall-clock budget to abort whichever provider is currently in-flight). Only `AbortSignal` propagates from the use-case down to the network read. Use `timeout` as belt-and-braces if desired, but `AbortSignal` is load-bearing.
3. **OpenAI strict-mode `json_schema` rejects schemas without `additionalProperties: false`** — When using the manual `response_format: { type: 'json_schema', strict: true }` fallback (instead of `zodResponseFormat`), the JSON Schema MUST set `additionalProperties: false` on every object node. Zod 4's `z.toJSONSchema()` does this by default (strips unknown keys), so prefer it over hand-rolled schemas.
4. **Plant.id `details.common_names[]` may not include pt-BR for every species** — Even with `?language=pt`, less common Brazilian flora returns an empty/missing common-names list. Fall back to `commonName: null` (PRD §17 anti-fake-precision) — do NOT fabricate a Portuguese common name from English data, and do NOT silently drop the result.
5. **Zod parse failure on a novel provider response variant** — When Plant.id ships a new field or OpenAI-compat returns a malformed JSON, `Schema.parse()` throws. This MUST surface as `failure_reason=invalid_response` (D-15) and increment the breaker (D-10). It MUST NOT be retried inside the connector (no retry loop) and MUST NOT be silently swallowed; the router decides whether to fall over to the next provider.
6. **Plant.id rate limits and credit-tier pricing** — VERIFY current limits and credit pricing at deploy time. Entry tier (~€0.05/credit) means the $5/day Plant.id ceiling caps at ~90 calls/day; a higher-volume tier or a swap to OpenAI-compat-only fits more calls. Surface this asymmetry in operator dashboards (later phase) — for MVP, the priority router and ceilings handle it.
7. **Base64-in-JSON for Plant.id vs. multipart trade-off** — Plant.id v3 accepts both `application/json` with base64 and `multipart/form-data` with file parts. Base64 is ~33% bigger over the wire. For 1MB-compressed images and the 50s budget, base64 is fine and simpler; if a higher-res future flow ships, switch to multipart per the v3 docs.

**Model Configuration:**

- **Plant.id (primary, `priority=10`):** `POST https://api.plant.id/v3/identification` with header `Api-Key: ${PLANT_ID_API_KEY}`. Body `{ images: [base64...], classification_level: 'species', similar_images: false }`. Query `?details=common_names&language=pt` for pt-BR common names. No prompt, no temperature — it is a classifier, not an LLM. Per-call ≈ €0.05 at the 1k-credit entry tier; seed `ProviderBudget.cost_per_call_cents = 6`. // VERIFY current Plant.id v3 endpoint + pricing tier at deploy time.
- **OpenAI-compat (fallback, `priority=20`):** `model='gpt-4o-mini'` (May 2026 — vendor-neutral compatible endpoint); `temperature=0` (deterministic extraction); `max_tokens=600` (caps output cost — never unbounded in production); `response_format=zodResponseFormat(TopThreeSchema, 'top3_species')` (strict JSON Schema with `additionalProperties: false` enforced by Zod 4 → JSON Schema conversion); image content blocks use `detail: 'low'` since the 1MB client-side compress per D-28 already keeps images small enough that high-detail vision tokens would be wasted. // VERIFY: confirm the chosen OpenAI-compatible vendor's exact model identifier at deploy time — gpt-4o-mini is the OpenAI canonical name; a vendor-neutral compat endpoint may use a renamed alias.

**Core Pattern:**

```typescript
// src/contexts/identification/api/identifications-route.ts (sketch)
export async function POST(req: Request) {
  const startedAt = Date.now();
  const TOTAL_BUDGET_MS = 50_000;
  const remainingMs = () => TOTAL_BUDGET_MS - (Date.now() - startedAt);
  const outerController = new AbortController();
  const outerTimer = setTimeout(() => outerController.abort(), TOTAL_BUDGET_MS);
  try {
    const userId = await requireAuthedUser(req);
    posthog.capture({ distinctId: userId, event: 'identification_started' });   // D-32

    const photos = await validateIdentificationPhotos(req);                     // D-28 (GPS/MIME/size)
    const cap = await checkCap(userId);                                         // D-12; BEFORE counter (COST-03)
    if (!cap.allowed) return capHitResponse(cap);                               // 429, no provider call
    if (!(await hasActiveConsent(userId, 'identification_third_party'))) {
      return error403('consent_required');                                      // IDENT-03
    }
    const consentVersion = await activeConsentVersion(userId, 'identification_third_party');

    const providers = await loadActiveProviders('identification');              // ORDER BY priority (D-04)
    for (const provider of providers) {
      if (remainingMs() < 10_000) break;                                        // D-06 fallover guard (IDENT-18)
      if (await breakerOpen(provider.providerId)) continue;                     // D-08 skip
      const reserved = await reservePreflight(provider, provider.estimateCost({...})); // D-05 atomic
      if (!reserved) continue;                                                  // ceiling reached → next provider
      const callCtl = new AbortController();
      outerController.signal.addEventListener('abort', () => callCtl.abort());
      const callTimer = setTimeout(() => callCtl.abort(), Math.min(30_000, remainingMs()));
      const idRowId = await insertIdentificationInProgress({                    // D-13 pre-write
        userId, providerId: provider.providerId, consentVersion, photoUrls,
      });
      try {
        const { canonical, raw } = await provider.identify({                    // D-01 contract
          photos: photos.bytes, signal: callCtl.signal,
        });
        const filtered = canonical.results
          .filter((r) => r.confidence >= provider.minConfidence)                // IDENT-04
          .sort((a, b) => b.confidence - a.confidence)
          .slice(0, 3);
        await markIdentificationSuccess(idRowId, { results: raw, canonical: filtered });
        await maybeAlert80pct(provider.providerId);                             // D-30 one-shot per UTC day
        await emitInngest('identification.succeeded', { id: idRowId });
        posthog.capture({ distinctId: userId, event: 'identification_succeeded' });
        return jsonResponse({ id: idRowId, results: filtered });
      } catch (err) {
        const reason = mapInternalReason(err, callCtl.signal.aborted);          // timeout|invalid_response|...
        await markIdentificationFailed(idRowId, reason);
        if (reason === 'invalid_response' || reason === 'timeout' || reason === 'http_5xx') {
          await incrementBreaker(provider.providerId);                          // D-10 fault counts
        }
        // continue to next provider
      } finally { clearTimeout(callTimer); }
    }
    return error503('provider_unavailable');                                    // IDENT-12 (closed registry)
  } finally { clearTimeout(outerTimer); }
}
```

**Tool Use:** N/A. This is single-shot structured extraction — no agent, no tool calls, no function calling. The only "tool" is the choice of which provider to dispatch to, and that decision is made by the router (D-04 priority + D-05 ceiling skip + D-08 breaker), not by the LLM.

**State Management:** Stateless per call. There is no conversation history to carry, no session, no cache of prior identifications. The only durable state is:

- `Identification` row (one per user request — D-07, pre-written `status='in_progress'` per D-13, updated to `success`/`failed`)
- `ProviderUsageCounter` row (atomic increment per D-05 + D-11; persists for the UTC day)
- `provider_breaker_states` row (D-08; module-level cache per Lambda fronts the DB)
- Photo bytes in Supabase storage (`plant-photos` bucket, refs in `photo_urls TEXT[]` per D-26/D-27)

The use-case reads cap/budget/breaker state via the D-14/D-15 in-memory TTL cache and writes through to DB on every state transition. No Redis, no Vercel KV — module-level `Map` per Lambda is the cache (D-14).

**Context Window Strategy:** Effectively non-existent for this system.

- **Plant.id:** Not an LLM — no context window. Token / context concepts do not apply. The only size knob is image count and base64 size; the 1MB client compress per D-28 keeps both bounded.
- **OpenAI-compat:** Vision input is image-token bound, not text-token bound. With `detail: 'low'` and 1MB client-compressed images, expect ~1k–3k input tokens per photo + ~300 output tokens cap (`max_tokens=600` is the hard ceiling). System prompt is short (~150 tokens) and static. No conversation history. No RAG retrieval. No long context. Standard `gpt-4o-mini` 128k window is wildly over-provisioned for this use case — context-management complexity is N/A.
- **Server-side image preprocessing:** None beyond MIME / GPS / size validation per D-28. The client owns compression and EXIF/GPS strip per CLAUDE.md image pipeline; the server is defense-in-depth only.

**Retry policy on validation failure — DO NOT retry inside the connector.** Per D-10 the lock is: `invalid_response` faults count toward the breaker. A silent in-connector retry would (a) double-bill the user against `ProviderUsageCounter`, (b) hide a real provider regression behind retry success, and (c) lose the breaker signal that protects budget. Concrete policy:

1. One Zod parse attempt per provider call.
2. On `ZodError`, the connector throws — the use-case maps to `failure_reason=invalid_response` (IDENT-15) and writes the row.
3. The router increments the breaker (D-10) and falls over to the next provider in priority order (D-04), provided ≥10s remain (D-06).
4. Log the `ZodError.issues` to Sentry breadcrumb for diagnosis; never include the user's raw image bytes in the breadcrumb.

### Async-First Design

- **Promises over events.** TypeScript `async`/`await` throughout the connector → use-case → route handler. Inngest is for `notifications/email.requested` (D-30) and `identification.succeeded` (post-success), not for the synchronous identification request itself.
- **AbortSignal is the cancellation primitive — not `Promise.race` against a timer.** Racing a fetch against `setTimeout` does not cancel the fetch; the network read continues, the connection holds, and the function memory persists past the 50s budget. Use `AbortController` + `signal` and let the runtime garbage-collect the rejected fetch promise. The pattern is one outer 50s controller (wall-clock) + one 30s child controller per provider call (per IDENT-13 / D-06), with the outer's `abort` event linked to the child:

```typescript
const outerController = new AbortController();
setTimeout(() => outerController.abort(), 50_000);
// per-provider-call:
const callCtl = new AbortController();
outerController.signal.addEventListener('abort', () => callCtl.abort());
setTimeout(() => callCtl.abort(), Math.min(30_000, remainingMs()));
```

- **Sequential, not parallel, provider calls.** The fallover order is by `ProviderBudget.priority` (D-04) — Plant.id first, OpenAI-compat second only if the primary skipped or failed. `Promise.all` would burn both providers' budget on every request and waste the second's quota.
- **Stream vs. await.** `await` for both connectors. Streaming gains nothing here — the response is small JSON, the UI is gated on validated structured output (the confidence-ladder needs the full top-3 to render), and streaming JSON token-by-token would defeat the Zod parse contract.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

Applies to OpenAI-compat ONLY. Plant.id has no prompt — it is a vision classifier with fixed taxonomy.

- **System vs. user separation.** System prompt holds the role + output contract + the anti-fake-precision instruction. User content is `[{ type: 'text', text: 'Identify this plant.' }, ...image_url blocks]`. The system prompt is versioned with the deploy (constant in code, not DB-driven) per IDENT-19.
- **Language.** System prompt in **English**. Reasoning: gpt-4o-mini's instruction-following is strongest in English; the structured JSON output it emits is language-agnostic, and the pt-BR instruction is targeted at the `commonName` field only, not at any reasoning text the user will see. The user-facing UI literals are pt-BR (handled in the React layer, not the prompt).
- **Anti-fake-precision (PRD §17).** The system prompt MUST instruct: "Confidence is a calibrated 0..1 probability, NOT a fabricated marketing number" and "If you are uncertain, return fewer items or set confidence below 0.30". The backend then applies the `min_confidence=0.30` filter (IDENT-04) — defense in depth.
- **No fabricated common names.** Explicit prompt clause: "Prefer pt-BR common names; if no Brazilian Portuguese common name exists, set commonName to null — do NOT invent one." Mirrors the Plant.id `commonName: null` fallback for symmetry across providers.
- **Few-shot.** None at MVP. The Zod-derived JSON Schema response_format already constrains output structure; in-context examples would inflate input tokens (cost) without changing well-defined extraction behaviour. Revisit only if eval flags consistent format violations.
- **`max_tokens`.** Always set explicitly. `max_tokens=600` for OpenAI-compat (≈ 3 results × ~200 tokens of structured JSON, well-padded). Never leave unbounded — a hung-output token loop on a $0.60/M-output model can multiply cost by 100x in seconds.

### Context Window Management

This system is single-shot stateless — context-management complexity is structurally absent. Concrete posture for each layer:

- **Image preprocessing:** None server-side. Client-side strip + ≤1MB compression per D-28 + EXIF/GPS validator (`rejectGpsMetadata` from `src/shared/images/server-validate.ts`) is the only image handling. No resizing, no thumbnailing, no format conversion in the connector.
- **Conversation history:** None. Each `POST /v1/identifications` is a fresh request. No multi-turn interaction with either provider.
- **RAG / retrieval:** N/A. Phase 6 has no document retrieval, no vector store, no embeddings. Care-guide content joins server-side AFTER selection by Species ID (Phase 7), not as context to the identification call.
- **Truncation / summarisation:** N/A — the system prompt is ~150 tokens, the schema-derived response_format adds ~200 tokens, image inputs are bounded by client compression. The 128k context window of `gpt-4o-mini` is over-provisioned by ~99%.

### Cost and Latency Budget

**Per-call cost order of magnitude (May 2026, VERIFY at deploy):**

- Plant.id v3 — flat-rate per identification credit: ~€0.05 at the 1k-credit entry tier, dropping to ~€0.012 at 800k+ credits. At the entry tier, the $5/day per-provider ceiling caps at **~90 calls/day**.
- OpenAI-compat (`gpt-4o-mini`, May 2026) — $0.15/1M input + $0.60/1M output. With 1MB client-compressed images at `detail: 'low'` (~1.5k input tokens/photo) + ~300 output tokens, single-photo calls are ≈ $0.0005–$0.001; multi-photo (3-5 images) ≈ $0.002–$0.005. The $5/day per-provider ceiling caps at **~1,000–2,500 calls/day** depending on photo count.

**The asymmetry matters.** Plant.id at entry tier is ~10x more expensive per call than OpenAI-compat. The router's priority ordering (Plant.id `priority=10` first, OpenAI-compat `priority=20` second) is a correctness choice (Plant.id has higher domain accuracy on Brazilian flora, hence primary), not a cost choice. Operators MAY flip the ordering in `provider_budgets.priority` if Plant.id quality is acceptable at the OpenAI-compat price floor — the adapter pattern (D-02) supports it without code change.

**Latency budget:** total wall-clock 50s (D-06), per-call cap 30s (D-06), fallover requires ≥10s remaining (IDENT-18). Plant.id p50 is sub-3s for typical loads; OpenAI-compat vision p50 is 4–10s with `detail: 'low'`. Sequential primary-then-fallback fits comfortably; parallel calls are ruled out (cost asymmetry above).

**Caching strategy: NONE.** Every user upload is a unique photo. There is no cache-key candidate — neither exact-match (every byte stream differs) nor semantic (would need an embedding pipeline that doesn't exist in this phase and would itself cost money). The only "caching" in scope is the budget/breaker state cache (D-14/D-15) — not response cache.

**Cheaper-model routing for sub-tasks:** N/A. There is one task (top-3 classification) and the priority router is the routing layer. There is no classification step before the classification step.

**Operational safety nets that protect the cost ceiling:**

1. Atomic preflight reservation (D-05) — `WHERE current + estimate <= ceiling` is the single source of truth; race conditions cannot blow it.
2. 80% operator alert (COST-04 / D-30) — one Resend email per provider per UTC day at 80% of `daily_cost_cap_cents`.
3. Per-user caps (COST-01/02) — trial 5/75, paid 15/200; a single abusive user cannot exhaust the daily budget alone.
4. Circuit breaker (D-08–D-10) — a misbehaving provider auto-skipped after 5 consecutive faults in 5 min; cooldown 120s, half-open 1 probe.

| 1 | **Top-3 recall on common Brazilian houseplants & natives** | PASS: Expert-labelled correct species appears at any rank within the top-3 returned by the provider (post-`min_confidence` filter) for ≥80% of golden-set positive examples. FAIL: Correct species absent from top-3, or top-3 is empty when the photo clearly shows a confidently-identifiable common houseplant ("textbook Costela-de-Adão" → empty result). | **Code** — exact / accepted-alias match (`scientificName` ∈ `expectedScientificName ∪ allowedAlternatives`) against `tests/eval/identification/golden.json`. Sansevieria↔Dracaena alias pair is explicit per Section 1b §"Sansevieria → Dracaena legacy taxonomy confusion". | **High** | FM #4 (cascades to wrong care) |
| 2 | **Top-1 precision in the high-confidence band (≥0.70)** | PASS: When the top-1 result has `confidence ≥ 0.70`, the species is correct (or an accepted taxonomic alias) on ≥95% of golden-set positives. FAIL: Any case where the top-1 is wrong AND `confidence ≥ 0.70` — this is the PRD §17 anti-fake-precision violation that beginners over-trust. | **Code** — boolean check on the (correct, confidence ≥ 0.70) intersection across the golden set. Single failure on the toxic-look-alike subset is a Critical regression, not a Medium one. | **Critical** | FM #3 (over-confident wrong) |
| 3 | **Confidence calibration (Brier score + reliability diagram)** | PASS: Brier score ≤ 0.15 across the golden set; reliability diagram shows the 0.70–1.00 band's empirical accuracy is within ±0.10 of stated confidence (i.e., 70% claimed → 60–80% observed). FAIL: Brier > 0.20, OR the high-confidence band's empirical accuracy is below 0.60 (the band is noise), OR all confidences cluster in 0.85–0.95 regardless of input quality (uncalibrated softmax — exact failure mode flagged in Section 1b §"Honest uncertainty"). | **Code** — custom TS script computing Brier + binned reliability against golden set. Plain Vitest test, no library. Outputs `tests/eval/identification/__output__/calibration-{date}.json` for trend tracking. | **Critical** | FM #3 (the confidence ladder is meaningless if its bands are noise) |
| 4 | **Scientific name canonicalisation** | PASS: Returned `scientificName` is a real binomial accepted by Flora e Funga do Brasil / Reflora / GBIF backbone — current accepted name OR a documented synonym (Sansevieria trifasciata ≡ Dracaena trifasciata is one accepted pair; the eval's `allowedAlternatives` list is the source of truth). FAIL: Fabricated/non-existent binomial; right genus + wrong species (e.g., `Monstera adansonii` returned for a clear `M. deliciosa` photo); single-word "name" missing genus. | **Code** — string membership check against the golden record's `expectedScientificName ∪ allowedAlternatives`. Synonym list maintained in `tests/eval/identification/synonyms.json`. **Future flywheel**: cross-reference with GBIF API for novel names that appear in production but not in the synonym list. | **High** | FM #4 (wrong species → wrong care guide) |
| 5 | **pt-BR common name authenticity** | PASS: Returned `commonName` is either (a) `null`, OR (b) a name attested in SiBBr / Reflora / Flora e Funga do Brasil vernacular indices for that species, OR (c) a name a Brazilian nursery would write on a plant tag (paisagista standard from Section 1b). FAIL: Fabricated pt-BR-sounding name not in any registry (the OpenAI fallback's primary risk per FM #2); English calque ("planta-cobra" instead of "espada-de-São-Jorge"); Portuguese-from-Portugal name where a distinct pt-BR name exists; non-null name attached to the wrong species in Brazilian usage. | **LLM judge** (calibrated against Brazilian botanist/jardineiro labels — target ≥ 0.7 Cohen's κ before trusting unsupervised). Prompt provides the SiBBr/Reflora vernacular shortlist for the candidate species + asks "would a Brazilian nursery write this on a plant tag for *this* species, or is it fabricated?". Periodic spot-checks by the panel of beta users (Section 1b expert table). | **Critical** | FM #2 (fabricated common name) |
| 6 | **Toxic-look-alike discrimination (Araceae subset)** | PASS: On the curated Dieffenbachia + similar-non-toxic-aroid pairs, the system either returns the correct toxic species at any confidence in top-3, OR returns no result; it MUST NOT return a non-toxic Philodendron at confidence ≥ 0.50 when the photo is a Dieffenbachia (or vice-versa). Genus-level correctness within Araceae is the floor; species-level is the goal. FAIL: Any cross-toxicity-boundary mis-id at confidence ≥ 0.50 — this is the household-safety failure flagged in Section 1b §"Toxicity-relevant species discrimination" and FM #1. | **Code** subset eval — the toxic-pair lookup table from the veterinary-toxicologist consult is a hard constraint check (`expectedToxicityClass` field on each golden record; cross-class with confidence ≥ 0.50 = hard fail). One single hard fail blocks the eval suite from passing. | **Critical** | FM #1 (toxic look-alike) |
| 7 | **Native-vs-ornamental discrimination** | PASS: On Brazilian-native-vs-foreign-cultivar pairs in the golden set (Anthurium / Heliconia / Bromeliad genus / Pau-d'Água examples), top-3 contains the correct genus and the correct species concept (cultivar level is acceptable to omit per Section 1b §"Cultivar / variegation invisibility"). FAIL: Brazilian native returned as a foreign cultivar (or reverse) at top-1 with confidence ≥ 0.70 — drives wrong watering cadence in Phase 8 per FM #4. | **Code** subset eval — same scientific-name-match logic as Dimension 4, restricted to the `nativeVsOrnamental: true` subset of the golden set. Pass threshold: ≥ 75% (lower than Dimension 1 because vision models trained on European/NA data underperform on natives — Section 1b §"Brazilian-native genus confusion"). | **High** | FM #4 (cascades to wrong care) |
| 8 | **Non-plant input rejection** | PASS: On the negative-class subset (furniture, plastic plant, fabric with plant pattern, magazine cover), the system returns zero results post-`min_confidence=0.30` filter on ≥80% of cases. FAIL: Any negative-class input returns a confident (≥ 0.50) species classification — the model "hallucinates" a plant where none exists, exactly as flagged in FM #5 and Section 1b §"Non-plant inputs". | **Code** — boolean check on `results.length === 0` for `isNonPlant: true` golden records. Failures here directly invalidate the `min_confidence=0.30` floor (IDENT-04) and trigger a router/threshold review. | **High** | FM #5 (non-plant returned as plant) |

**Coverage matrix.** Critical Failure Modes #1–#5 are each covered by ≥1 Critical-or-High dimension. FM #3 is covered by two dimensions (#2 and #3) because it is the most-cited launch-blocker concern in PRD §17. Dimension priorities are set so that a single Critical-priority failure on the production reference run blocks deploy; High-priority failures alert without blocking; trend regressions on any dimension feed the offline flywheel (Section 6).

### Eval Tooling

**Primary tools:**

- **Promptfoo** — CI/CD prompt-regression and golden-dataset replay. CLI-first, no platform account required, fits the project's TypeScript stack and GitHub-Actions-only deploy posture (CLAUDE.md). Runs both connectors (mocked + live) against the golden dataset; emits a JSON report consumed by the GitHub Actions check.
- **Custom Vitest eval script** — Brier score + reliability-diagram computation for Dimension 3. The dataset is small enough (30 examples target, 10–15 in Phase 6 ship) that a 50-line TS script computing per-bin empirical accuracy is more honest than pulling in a metrics library. Lives at `tests/eval/identification/calibration.test.ts`.
- **No tracing platform shipped in Phase 6.** See "Honest call on Arize Phoenix" below — the existing PostHog + Sentry stack covers single-call observability without adding new infrastructure that would conflict with the Vercel-only deploy posture.

**Honest call on Arize Phoenix (the workflow's stated default):** Phoenix is *additive only*, not adopted in Phase 6. The system has no chain / agent / multi-step trace to visualise — it is a single `connector.identify()` call wrapped in a router. The trace value Phoenix is built for (multi-span LLM workflows) does not exist here. PostHog event properties (`identification_started`, `identification_succeeded`, `identification_failed` with `provider_id`, `confidence_band`, `failure_reason`, `duration_ms`) plus Sentry breadcrumbs on connector failures already provide the observability surface a small-scale plant-ID system needs. **Recommendation:** revisit Arize Phoenix only when (a) identifications exceed ~1,000/week AND (b) we add a multi-step pipeline (e.g., a pre-classifier or a post-rerank stage that creates real spans worth visualising). Until then, Phoenix would be overhead without payoff — and self-hosted Phoenix requires Docker, which conflicts with the Vercel-only deploy lock in CLAUDE.md.

**Schema** (`tests/eval/identification/golden.json` — JSON file, not a database):

```typescript
type GoldenRecord = {
  id: string;                                      // stable identifier
  photoPath: string;                               // path under tests/eval/identification/photos/
  expectedScientificName: string | null;           // null for isNonPlant: true
  expectedCommonNamePtBr: string | null;           // null when no canonical pt-BR name exists
  allowedAlternatives: string[];                   // accepted taxonomic synonyms (e.g., Sansevieria↔Dracaena)
  expectedToxicityClass?: 'toxic' | 'non-toxic';   // present for the toxic-look-alike subset (Dimension 6)
  nativeVsOrnamental?: boolean;                    // true for Dimension 7 subset
  isNonPlant?: boolean;                            // true for Dimension 8 negative class
  notes?: string;                                  // expert annotation (for calibration / debugging)
  labelledBy: 'founder' | 'botanist' | 'jardineiro' | 'vet-toxicologist';
  labelledAt: string;                              // ISO-8601 UTC
};
```

**Labeling:**

- **Phase 6 ship (10–15 examples):** Founder-labelled first cut covering the **Common Brazilian houseplants** subset (10 records) plus a small starter from **Non-plant negative class** (3 records) — these are the easiest to verify against SiBBr/Reflora and Wikipedia common-name pages without specialist consultation, and they unblock the Promptfoo + calibration scripts in CI on day one.
- **Follow-up (the remaining ~15 examples):** Brazilian botanist consult labels the **Brazilian natives** + **Cultivar/variegation** + **Ambiguous/diseased** subsets. Veterinary toxicologist labels the **Toxic-look-alike pairs** subset (one-time consult per Section 1b expert table). Jardineiro / paisagista reviews all `expectedCommonNamePtBr` fields for nursery-tag-real authenticity.
- **LLM-judge calibration:** Once the botanist + jardineiro labels are in, the Dimension 5 LLM judge is calibrated against their labels (target Cohen's κ ≥ 0.7) before being trusted unsupervised on production sampling.
- **Photo storage:** Photos themselves are committed under `tests/eval/identification/photos/` as JPEG/PNG ≤ 1MB each (matches the production D-28 client compression cap). Source preference: founder's own photos, CC0 photos from Wikimedia, and beta-user-donated photos with explicit eval-use consent (LGPD Art. 33 — separate consent purpose from production identification).

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

> All five guardrails below are **already in scope** for Phase 6 per the SPEC / Decisions — this section documents how each one functions as an evaluation guardrail, not new work.

| # | Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention | Source Lock |
| - | --------- | ------- | ------------ | ----------- |
| 1 | **`min_confidence` floor filter** | Any provider result with `confidence < ProviderBudget.min_confidence` (default 0.30) | **Drop** the result before returning to client. If all results are dropped, the API returns `{ results: [] }` — the UI's "Não encontramos seu vegetal" zero-results state (D-22) renders. Single line of defense against FM #5 (non-plant returned as plant) and the over-confidence band of FM #3. | IDENT-04 |
| 2 | **Top-3 hard cap** | Provider returns >3 results | **Truncate** to top 3 after sort-by-confidence-descending. Prevents the UI from rendering a >3-card layout that breaks the equal-prominence rule (D-24) and prevents result-count drift from inflating downstream cost in Phase 7. | IDENT-04 |
| 3 | **Zod schema validation** | Provider response fails `PlantIdResponseSchema.parse()` or OpenAI `parsed` is empty | **Throw** → use-case writes `failure_reason=invalid_response` (IDENT-15) → router increments breaker (D-10) → falls over to next provider if budget allows. NO retry inside connector (per Section 4b "Retry policy"). | D-10, D-15 |
| 4 | **Cost ceiling skip (atomic preflight)** | Provider's `current + estimateCost(input) > daily_cost_cap_cents` for the UTC day | **Skip** the provider before any HTTP call (`reservePreflight` returns false). Router moves to next provider; if all providers exhausted, returns `provider_unavailable` (IDENT-12). The single most important guardrail for cost — race-free, atomic, no Postgres lock during HTTP. | D-05, COST-09 |
| 5 | **LGPD consent gate** | User has no active `identification_third_party` consent for the current `policy_version` | **Block** with `403 consent_required` (IDENT-03) BEFORE any provider call. Re-consent required after `policy_version` bump (e.g., vendor swap). Defensive against unauthorised international data transfer per LGPD Art. 33. | IDENT-03 |

**Out of Phase 6 scope (cross-phase reference, do NOT ship in Phase 6):**

- **Toxic-plant warning interstitial** — the UI surfacing of "this species is toxic; raphides cause oral irritation in children/pets" belongs in Phase 7 (CareGuide rendering). Phase 6 only ensures the *species selection* is correct; the *toxicity surfacing* is downstream. The eval Dimension 6 (toxic-look-alike discrimination) is the Phase 6 control point that protects the Phase 7 warning from being attached to a wrong species.

### Offline (Flywheel)

| # | Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
| - | ------ | ----------------- | --------------------- |
| 1 | **Daily PostHog rollup: identification outcomes by `(provider_id, confidence_band)`** | Inngest cron (matches the `Inngest cron rollups` mentioned in CLAUDE.md observability) reads PostHog API daily, computes win-rate / fall-through-rate / fallover-rate / zero-results-rate per provider and per confidence-ladder band (`low`/`medium`/`high`). Stored in a small `identification_quality_rollup` table for 90-day trend retention. | A 7-day rolling drop in win-rate >10pp, OR a fallover-rate >25% over 7 days, opens a Sentry issue tagged `team:eval` and surfaces in the founder's Monday review. |
| 2 | **Weekly golden-dataset replay against current provider config** | GitHub Actions scheduled workflow (`.github/workflows/eval-identification-weekly.yml`) runs the full Promptfoo + calibration suite against **live** providers using the production `ProviderBudget` config (priority + min_confidence). Cost per run is ≤30 calls × ~$0.02 mixed = ~$0.60 — negligible. | Any Critical-priority dimension regression vs. last week's baseline → blocks the next deploy. High-priority regression → PR comment + Slack ping, no block. Calibration drift (Brier > 0.20) → manual review of recent prompt or model changes. |
| 3 | **Sampled human review of selected results that did NOT trigger manual-correction** | Weekly random sample of 10 successful identifications where the user *selected* a result and did *not* use the "Não é nenhuma destas?" affordance within 24h. Founder reviews against the photo (stored ≤30d per LGPD posture); flags suspected silent misidentifications. Confirmed silent misses become new golden-set records. | Each confirmed silent miss adds one record to the golden set (composition stays balanced — caps each subset at the target counts above). A run of >2 silent misses in one weekly review opens a Sentry issue and prompts a confidence-calibration audit. |
| 4 | **Sampled review of low-confidence selections** | Weekly random sample of 5 cases where the user selected a low-band (`<0.40`) confidence result. Did they keep the Plant 7 days later, or did they delete? "Kept" = positive signal the low-confidence ID was right (low-band recall is working); "deleted" = the low band is too generous (raise `min_confidence`?). | Persistent "deleted within 7 days" rate >50% on low-band selections → operator considers raising `ProviderBudget.min_confidence` to 0.40 for the affected provider (DB-driven hot-reload per D-15). |

**The flywheel principle:** every offline metric above produces either a golden-set expansion (Metric 3) or a `ProviderBudget` config tuning candidate (Metric 4) or a regression alert (Metrics 1, 2). The system improves by tightening the production guardrails based on production signal — no new code paths required, just config (D-15 hot-reload) and dataset (Metric 3 expansions).

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing tool:** **PostHog event properties + Sentry breadcrumbs (already in stack — no new infra in Phase 6).** Arize Phoenix is explicitly *not adopted* — see Section 5 "Honest call on Arize Phoenix" for rationale. Recommendation revisited only when identifications exceed ~1,000/week AND a multi-step pipeline emerges; until then, the existing observability stack covers single-call traces honestly.

PostHog event taxonomy for the identification flow (already defined in CLAUDE.md `Sentry.setUser({ id })` constraint — no email in user property; PII-safe):

- `identification_started` — `{ provider_id_attempted_first, photo_count }`
- `identification_succeeded` — `{ provider_id, duration_ms, top1_confidence, top1_confidence_band, result_count, fallover_count, consent_version }`
- `identification_failed` — `{ provider_id_last, failure_reason, duration_ms, fallover_count }`
- `identification_cap_hit` — `{ cap_type: 'per_user_daily' | 'per_user_monthly' }`
- `identification_breaker_skip` — `{ provider_id, breaker_state }`
- `identification_manual_correction_used` — `{ provider_id, top1_confidence_band, selected_rank | 'none-of-these' }`

Sentry breadcrumbs are written on every connector failure (`ZodError.issues`, HTTP status, `aborted` flag) with the user's image bytes EXCLUDED per Section 4b retry-policy clause 4.

**Key Metrics to Track (5):**

| # | Metric | Source | What it tells us |
| - | ------ | ------ | ---------------- |
| 1 | `identification_provider_latency_p50` / `p95` per `provider_id` | PostHog `identification_succeeded.duration_ms` rollup | Provider health; p95 trending up = vendor degradation, may force fallover sooner |
| 2 | `identification_fallover_rate` (% of `succeeded` events with `fallover_count > 0`) | PostHog | Plant.id reliability proxy; high rate = primary provider issues OR Plant.id budget exhaustion (cross-check with daily cost) |
| 3 | `identification_zero_results_rate` (% of requests where final returned `results.length === 0` after `min_confidence` filter) | PostHog `identification_succeeded` with `result_count = 0` (route returns 200 + empty array per D-22, NOT a failure) | Confidence-calibration drift indicator; if vendor model shifts and confidences sag below 0.30, this rate spikes — eval Dimension 3 calibration would also degrade in lockstep |
| 4 | `identification_manual_correction_rate` (% of `succeeded` followed by `manual_correction_used` within 1h) | PostHog funnel | **Direct ground-truth signal of identification quality** — the user is voting "this is wrong" with their fingers. Most important production-quality metric in this system. |
| 5 | `identification_breaker_open_minutes_per_day` per `provider_id` | `provider_breaker_states` table aggregated daily by Inngest cron | Provider reliability over time; persistent open state for either provider = vendor SLA review or swap discussion |

**Alert Thresholds:**

| # | Alert | Threshold | Channel | Severity |
| - | ----- | --------- | ------- | -------- |
| 1 | **80% provider budget alert** (already in scope per COST-04 / D-30) | `ProviderUsageCounter.estimated_cost_cents ≥ 0.80 × daily_cost_cap_cents` for any provider on any UTC day | Resend email to operator address (one-shot per provider per UTC day) | Info |
| 2 | **Circuit breaker stuck open** | `breaker_open_minutes_per_day` > 30 min for any provider | Sentry issue + email to founder (MVP = on-call) | High |
| 3 | **Manual-correction rate regression** | `identification_manual_correction_rate` > 25% on a 7-day rolling window | Sentry issue tagged `team:eval`; surfaces in Monday review | High — quality regression, investigate prompt / model / golden set drift |
| 4 | **Zero-results rate regression** | `identification_zero_results_rate` > 15% on a 7-day rolling window | Sentry issue tagged `team:eval` | High — confidence calibration drift; cross-check with weekly golden replay (Section 6 Flywheel #2) |
| 5 | **Fallover rate spike** | `identification_fallover_rate` > 40% on a 7-day rolling window | Sentry issue + check daily Plant.id cost | Medium — Plant.id reliability OR budget; if cost is at ceiling, this is expected; if cost is sub-50%, vendor issue |
| 6 | **Per-provider hard outage** | 5+ consecutive `identification_failed` events with `failure_reason=http_5xx` for one provider in 5 minutes | Sentry critical issue + founder pager (mirrors breaker open trigger from D-09) | Critical |

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**

The system has no high-volume telemetry stream — sampling here is about which production interactions to *manually review* for golden-set expansion and qualitative quality calibration, not about traffic-cost reduction. Five signal-driven samples, each cheap to review (1–2 minutes per record):

1. **"Selected then deleted within 24h"** — user picked a result and removed the Plant from "Meu Jardim" within a day. Strong proxy for "I realised the ID was wrong". Sample: 100% of these (volume should be small at MVP scale). Output: candidate golden-set additions for Dimensions 1, 2, 4. Founder review weekly.
2. **All `identification_cap_hit` events for the first 14 days post-launch** — verify the per-user cap math (trial 5/75, paid 15/200) is correct in real conditions; nothing worse than turning away paying users due to an off-by-one. Sample: 100% for 14 days, then drop to 10% steady-state. Channel: PostHog event filter + manual cross-check against `Identification` table.
3. **High-confidence band + manual correction used** — `top1_confidence_band='high'` AND `manual_correction_used` within 1h. These are the **over-confident wrong answers** (FM #3 / Dimension 2 — the launch-blocker risk). Sample: 100% for the first 90 days, indefinitely thereafter. Each one is a Critical-priority golden-set candidate. Founder + botanist review.
4. **5% random sample of zero-results across a 7-day window** — verify the model isn't silently failing to identify a confidently-real Brazilian common houseplant (e.g., a textbook Costela-de-Adão photo returning empty because of confidence-calibration drift). If the photo SHOULD have produced a confident result, that's a calibration regression — feeds Dimension 3.
5. **All `identification_failed` events with `failure_reason=invalid_response`** — these are the schema-parse failures (D-15) that increment the breaker. A run of these for one provider in a 24h window indicates a provider-side response-shape change; the connector schema needs updating. Sample: 100%, alert via Sentry breadcrumbs already wired.

**LGPD posture for sampling:** all sampling above operates on event metadata only (provider_id, confidence_band, failure_reason, IDs). Photo bytes themselves are NOT pulled into the founder's review surface; review happens via the Identify history detail screen (UI-12), which the founder accesses as a normal authenticated user with their own test account or with explicit user consent for a beta-program feedback flow. No raw user images leave the user's view scope without consent.
